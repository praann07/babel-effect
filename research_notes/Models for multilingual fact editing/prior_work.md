# Prior work: causal tracing, ROME/MEMIT editing, multilingual factual recall, cross-lingual edit transfer

Verification protocol: every arXiv ID below was opened at arxiv.org/abs/<id> in this session and title and authors were read off the page. Model, language and number details come from the arXiv HTML full text where I could read it. If only the abstract page was read, the note says so. ## Q1. Which papers study cross-lingual knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT, other), and what transfer rates, languages and models do they report?

### Takeaway
Every study found reports that parameter-editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, KN, FT, MEND) transfer poorly across languages, worse for non-Latin scripts and Indic languages. Only the Banerjee et al. paper (2406.11139) tests ROME/MEMIT directly on Hindi and Spanish, but on 7B Mistral/Tower/OpenHathi-style models, not Llama-3.2-3B. No paper found measures cross-language circuit overlap against a shuffled-fact null.

### Cited Findings

**Core cross-lingual editing papers (verified IDs)**
- Banerjee, Halder, Mandal, Layek, Soboroff, Hazra, Mukherjee, "Breaking Boundaries: Investigating the Effects of Model Editing on Cross-linguistic Performance", arXiv:2406.11139 (NAACL 2025 Industry). Tests ROME and MEMIT with "each language for itself" (ELFI) and "each language for others" (ELFO) strategies. Languages: English, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Hindi, Tamil, Kannada. Models: Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, TowerInstruct-7B-v0.2, OpenHathi-7B-Hi-v0.1-Base (Hindi), Tamil-Llama-7b-base-v0.1, Kan-LLaMA-7B-SFT. Data: about 550 CounterFact and 550 ZsRE instances, machine-translated; portability prompts rewritten with GPT-4. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11139), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4)
- Its numbers (exact/partial match, CounterFact reliability): Spanish self-edit, TowerInstruct ROME 0.82/0.92 and MEMIT 0.70/0.80; Mistral ROME 0.81/0.91 and MEMIT 0.78/0.86. Spanish with an English edit, Mistral ROME 0.57/0.62 and MEMIT 0.56/0.60. Hindi on OpenHathi, self-edit: ROME 0.02/0.02 and MEMIT 0.45/0.60. Hindi on OpenHathi, English edit: ROME 0.56/0.66 and MEMIT 0.02/0.03. On the merged Indic model, an English edit queried in English gives ROME 0.73/0.75 and MEMIT 0.95/0.95. Queried in Hindi it drops to ROME 0.00/0.00 and MEMIT 0.01/0.01. Tamil and Kannada are similarly near zero. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4). The numbers were extracted by a fetch-summarizer, so re-check the tables before citing them in a paper.
- The same paper says portability scores are "consistently low across all languages, models, and metrics" and that model merging does not close the gaps. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4)
- It lists layer 5 as the edit layer in the hyperparameter table (module `model.layers.{}.mlp.down_proj`, value loss at layer 31) but describes MEMIT as multi-layer. The paper is unclear here. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4)
- Wang, Liang, Sun, Cao, Xu, Meng, "Cross-Lingual Knowledge Editing in Large Language Models", arXiv:2309.08952 (ACL 2024). Translates ZsRE into Chinese and edits in English or Chinese. Models: Chinese-LLaMA-Plus-7B, Chinese-LLaMA-2-7B, Baichuan-7B, Baichuan2-7B. Methods: FT, SERAC, IKE, MEND, KN, ROME, MEMIT. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08952), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08952v2)
- On Chinese-LLaMA-Plus-7B, cross-lingual generality (F1/EM) is: ROME 26.65/6.75 for en-edit to zh-test and 24.24/8.87 for zh-edit to en-test. MEMIT is 28.26/6.75 and 22.88/8.97. KN is 6.66/0.00 and 4.74/0.00. IKE (in-context) is 92.95/69.72 and 85.39/77.24. The fetch-summarizer flagged possible column misalignment, so verify against the paper's tables. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08952v2)
- Khandelwal, Singh, Gu, Chen, Zhou, "Cross-Lingual Multi-Hop Knowledge Editing", arXiv:2407.10275 (Findings of EMNLP 2024). Introduces the CROLIN-MQUAKE benchmark and CLEVER-CKE. Models: ChatGPT (GPT-3.5-turbo-instruct), LLaMa-2-7B, Vicuna-1.5-7B. Languages: English, German, Spanish, Chinese, Russian, Hindi, Bengali, Swahili. FT, ROME and MEMIT "perform significantly worse" cross-lingually, with averages under 5.0%. MeLLo-CL accuracy is about 20% on Latin-script languages and about 11% on non-Latin scripts (Devanagari, Chinese, Cyrillic). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10275), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10275v2)
- Beniwal, Nandagopan D, Singh, "Cross-lingual Editing in Multilingual Language Models", arXiv:2401.10521 (EACL 2024). Cross-lingual model editing (XME) on BLOOM, mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa. Languages: English, French, Spanish (Latin) and Hindi, Gujarati, Bengali (Indic). The abstract reports notable limitations of editing methods under XME, "especially when the languages use different script families". Exact numbers not read. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10521)
- Qi, Fernandez, Bisazza, "Cross-Lingual Consistency of Factual Knowledge in Multilingual Language Models", arXiv:2310.10378 (EMNLP 2023). Case study edits with ROME in English on BLOOM-3b. Edits propagate to languages with high RankC with English (Spanish RankC 52, Vietnamese 49) and mostly not to low-RankC ones (Hungarian 26, Greek 24). Example, "Steve Jobs worked for": post-edit probability of the correct answer is 0.19 (en), 0.12 (es), 0.81 (hu), 0.91 (el). The paper notes the study is small. BMLAMA-17 includes Spanish; Hindi is not mentioned. Consistency analysis models: BLOOM 560m to 3b (7.1b in the appendix), mT5-large, XLM-R-large, with BLOOMZ-3b and LLaMA-7b in the appendix. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10378), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10378v5)
- Zhang, Liang, Meng, Zhang, Chen, Xu, Zhou, "Multilingual Knowledge Editing with Language-Agnostic Factual Neurons", arXiv:2406.16416 (COLING 2025). Proposes LU-LAFNs. Benchmarks: Bi-ZsRE (en, zh) and MzsRE (12 languages including Spanish; no Hindi). Models: Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2-7B, bloomz-7b1-mt (Llama2-7B in the appendix). Baselines: LoRA-FT, ReMaKE, M-ROME, M-MEMIT, M-PMET. Average over all metrics and both languages on Bi-ZsRE: LU-LAFNs 74.91, M-PMET 67.40, M-MEMIT 66.02, M-ROME 60.23, LoRA-FT 50.05, ReMaKE 46.89. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16416), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16416v2)
- Wang, Haddow, Birch, "Retrieval-augmented Multilingual Knowledge Editing" (ReMaKE), arXiv:2312.13040 (ACL 2024). Releases MzsRE (12 languages) and reports gains "up to +40.53%" in average accuracy. The abs page does not name models. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13040)
- Nie, Shao, Ding, Wang, Schmid, Schutze, "BMIKE-53: Investigating Cross-Lingual Knowledge Editing with In-Context Learning", arXiv:2406.17764 (ACL 2025). A 53-language benchmark combining zsRE, CounterFact and WikiFactDiff, evaluated with in-context editing (IKE) only. It reports that script type causes large performance differences, that non-Latin-script languages perform worse, partly from language confusion, and that larger models do better. Models not named on the abs page. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17764)
- Wei, Deng, Pang, Ding, Shen, Cheng, "MLaKE: Multilingual Knowledge Editing Benchmark for Large Language Models", arXiv:2404.04990. 4072 multi-hop and 5360 single-hop questions in English, Chinese, Japanese, French, German. Models not named on the abs page. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04990)
- Tamayo, Gonzalez-Agirre, Hernando, Villegas, "Mass-Editing Memory with Attention in Transformers: A cross-lingual exploration of knowledge" (MEMAT), arXiv:2502.02173. Model: Aguila-7B only, English and Catalan, CounterFact-derived data. No Hindi or Spanish. Does not report layer indices. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02173)
- Mousi, Durrani, Dalvi, "An Exploration of Knowledge Editing for Arabic", arXiv:2507.09629. ROME, MEMIT, ICE and LTE on Llama-2-7B-chat with Arabic ZsRE and CounterFact. Parameter-based methods generalize poorly across languages; instruction-tuned methods are more robust. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09629)
- Durrani, Mousi, Dalvi, "Editing Across Languages: A Survey of Multilingual Knowledge Editing", arXiv:2505.14393. Survey of parameter-, memory-, fine-tuning- and hypernetwork-based MKE, benchmarks and open problems (cross-lingual propagation, language anisotropy). Abstract page only; I did not read the body. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14393)

- mLAMA / Multilingual LAMA (Kassner, Dufter, Schutze), arXiv:2102.00894. TREx and GoogleRE translated into 53 languages, evaluated on mBERT. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00894)

### Inferences
- Hindi (OpenHathi) is the only direct ROME/MEMIT Hindi data point, and it is unstable: an English edit worked under ROME and failed under MEMIT, and the opposite held for a Hindi edit. That is consistent with edits being layer- and language-sensitive, not a clean "Hindi fails" story.
- In-context editing (IKE) transfers far better than weight editing (92.95 vs about 27 F1 on one backbone in 2309.08952). Parametric transfer is the hard case.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports ROME/MEMIT cross-lingual transfer on Llama-3.x (3.2-3B) in English, Spanish and Hindi together, and none reports perplexity damage per language. This is the open space for the proposed study.
- 2310.10378 uses BLOOM-3b and shows no Hindi. 2406.11139 uses OpenHathi (Llama-2-based, Hindi-adapted), not a general Llama.
- Exact numbers for BMIKE-53, MLaKE and ReMaKE models were not retrievable from abs pages. The 2312.13040 and 2406.17764 model lists were not read.

## Q2. Which papers study multilingual factual recall mechanisms (causal tracing, patching, language-agnostic vs language-specific neurons/layers, latent language), and what do they find about layers and Hindi/Indic?

### Takeaway
Findings converge on a three-stage picture: language-independent concept processing in the middle, then a language-specific output transition in the final layers. Most work is on English-centric models and says little about Hindi. No paper found runs causal tracing on Hindi.

### Cited Findings
- Wendler, Veselovsky, Monea, West, "Do Llamas Work in English? On the Latent Language of Multilingual Transformers", arXiv:2402.10588. Llama-2 family. Three phases: early layers far from the output embedding, middle layers can decode a semantically correct token but favor the English version, and final layers shift toward the input-language region ("input space", "concept space", "output space"). The concept space is closer to English than to other languages. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10588)
- Geva, Bastings, Filippova, Globerson, "Dissecting Recall of Factual Associations in Auto-Regressive Language Models", arXiv:2304.14767 (EMNLP 2023). Three-step mechanism: subject enrichment (often early MLPs), relation information propagation, then attribute extraction (usually attention heads). The abs page does not name models. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)
- Fierro, Foroutan, Elliott, Sogaard, "How Do Multilingual Language Models Remember Facts?", arXiv:2410.14387. Models: XGLM-7.5B (32 layers), EuroLLM-9B (42 layers), mT5-xl. Languages: en, es, vi, tr, ru, uk, ja, ko, he, fa, ar (the paper says "10" but lists 11). Data: mParaRel. Uses activation patching. Subject enrichment is language-independent, and object extraction is language-dependent. The last token acts as a function vector that encodes language and content. Unlike Meng et al. (GPT-2 XL, attention only at the late site), both MLPs and attention contribute at the late site in the multilingual models. No Hindi. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14387), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14387v3)
- Wang, Adel, Lange, Liu, Nie, Strotgen, Schutze, "Lost in Multilinguality: Dissecting Cross-lingual Factual Inconsistency in Transformer Language Models", arXiv:2504.04264. Models: LLaMA2-7B and BLOOM-560M. Uses logit lens, cross-language cosine similarity and information-flow dissection. Concept-space object extraction starts around layer 12 in LLaMA2 and layer 15 in BLOOM. The language transition happens at layer 28 in LLaMA2 and layer 19 in BLOOM. Latent similarity across languages peaks around 0.8 in the middle layers. A linear shortcut from layer 30 to the final layer raises LLaMA2 accuracy from 71.47 to 76.08 and consistency from 70.44 to 75.52. KLAR includes Spanish, not Hindi. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04264), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04264)
- Lu, Zhang, Eickhoff, Pavlick, "Paths Not Taken: Understanding and Mending the Multilingual Factual Recall Pipeline", arXiv:2505.20546. Main model: Llama-3.2-3B, the same model as the proposed study. Languages: English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, French, Spanish (no Hindi); 2,862 triples, 10 relation types. Non-English queries are routed through English-centric recall, then translated. Relation tokens reach the final position most clearly at layer 16 (43.30% for English prompts vs 32.65% multilingual). Answer extraction rises from layer 15 and peaks at layer 21. Translation is mainly in MLP layers 22-27. Vector interventions raise recall, for example Chinese from 2.39% to 41.83%. The paper notes its findings come from a single model, and its text and figure captions give slightly different layer numbers for some stages. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20546), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20546)
- Schut, Gal, Farquhar, "Do Multilingual LLMs Think In English?", arXiv:2502.15603. The abstract reports logit-lens evidence that decisions are made in English-like space and that steering works better with English-computed vectors. The HTML appendix runs causal tracing on a City Facts dataset (German, Dutch, French translations) with Aya-23-35B (main), Llama-3.1-70B, Mixtral-8x22B and Gemma-2-27b. Facts are "generally localized in similar layers, regardless of the language", with a mid-layer subject-token trace and a later attribute-recollection trace. The text gives no layer numbers (I read the first 100,000 of 123,218 characters). The share of generated words routed through English is 0.70 for Gemma-2-27b, 0.68 for Mixtral, 0.55 for Llama-3.1-70B, 0.50 for Aya-23-35B. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15603), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15603)
- Liu, Wang, Kargaran, Korner, Nie, Plank, Yvon, Schutze, "Tracing Multilingual Factual Knowledge Acquisition in Pretraining", arXiv:2505.14824 (EMNLP 2025 Findings). OLMo-7B checkpoints. Fact frequency drives acquisition language-agnostically, and cross-lingual transfer is limited and mostly early. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14824)
- Liu, Xiong, Schutze, "Evaluating Contextually Mediated Factual Recall in Multilingual Large Language Models", arXiv:2601.12555 (January 2026). Contextual mediation lowers recall in five languages; larger models are more robust. A behavioral paper. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12555)
- Zhang et al. (2406.16416) find language-agnostic factual neurons mostly in middle layers plus the last layer. Llama-3.1-8B peaks at layer 12 and Qwen2-7B at layer 18. LU-LAFNs edits Llama-3.1-8B at layers 11, 12, 13 and 31. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16416v2)
- Mondal, Sen, Singhania, Jyothi, "Language-specific Neurons Do Not Facilitate Cross-Lingual Transfer", arXiv:2503.17456. Llama 3.1 and Mistral Nemo. Neuron-based interventions bring no cross-lingual gains on XNLI and XQuAD. Languages not named on the abs page; a search snippet mentioned Hindi and Urdu, which I did not confirm. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17456)

### Inferences
- Llama-3.2-3B has 28 layers. The Lu et al. layer figures (relation propagation about 10-20, extraction peak 21, translation 22-27) come from an indexing I did not check, but they suggest recall sits in the middle to upper-middle of the stack. The 28-layer count comes from my background knowledge and was not verified here.
- Across 2410.14387, 2504.04264, 2505.20546 and 2502.15603 the pattern is: subject and relation processing is shared across languages, and language-specific divergence appears late. A circuit-overlap measurement would therefore be expected to show high overlap in middle layers and lower overlap near the output.

### Gaps
- No paper found does causal tracing or patching for Hindi, or for any Indic language, on a decoder LLM. Devanagari script tokenization effects on tracing are untreated.
- The number of layers and exact Llama-3.2-3B layer count were not verified against a model card.
- I did not read the 2502.15603 appendix figures, so Llama-3.1-70B per-layer localization is unquantified.

## Q3. Which papers report where factual recall is localized in Llama-family or Gemma-family models, and does it differ from the ROME GPT-J/GPT-2-XL mid-layer result?

### Takeaway
ROME's original result is mid-layer MLPs at the last subject token. For Llama/Gemma I found no paper that publishes a clean ROME-style causal-tracing layer for Llama-3 or Gemma that I could read. Related evidence suggests early to mid MLPs enrich the subject and later layers extract the answer, and multilingual models differ from GPT-2 XL in the late site.

### Cited Findings
- Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov, "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT" (ROME), arXiv:2202.05262. Mid-layer feed-forward modules process the subject and drive factual predictions. ROME edits those MLP weights. The abs page does not name models; GPT-2 XL and GPT-J are the models I know from the paper, which is not confirmed here. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)
- Meng, Sen Sharma, Andonian, Belinkov, Bau, "Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer" (MEMIT), arXiv:2210.07229. Edits thousands of facts at once. Models: GPT-J (6B) and GPT-NeoX (20B). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229)
- Fierro et al. contrast: in multilingual XGLM, EuroLLM and mT5 both MLPs and attention contribute at the late site, whereas Meng et al. found only attention there for GPT-2 XL. XGLM showed no early MLP site. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14387v3)
- Fang et al., "AlphaEdit: Null-Space Constrained Knowledge Editing for Language Models", arXiv:2410.02355 (ICLR 2025 oral). Tests LLaMA3, GPT2-XL and GPT-J and reports a 36.7% average improvement for locate-then-edit methods. The abs page gives no layer indices. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)
- Banerjee et al. use layer 5 as the edit layer for 7B models (Mistral-based and Llama-based). The layer 5 setting is a hyperparameter, not a measured tracing result. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4)
- Search results (not primary-source verified) report a Gemma-2-2B causal-tracing reproduction that edits layers [3,10], and a tutorial page showing Llama-3.2-1B with an early site near layer 3 of 16 and a final-position site by layer 9. Treat both as UNVERIFIED because I did not open them. — [Circuit-Diff listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23892), [NDIF tutorial](https://smithery.ai/skills/ndif-team/causal-tracing). I did open arXiv:2609.23892 and its abs page does not name any model, so the Gemma-2-2B claim is not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Layer-5 and layers 4-8 style edit settings are common defaults for Llama-class models (my background knowledge, UNVERIFIED here). The proposed study should measure its own tracing curve on Llama-3.2-3B and not assume the GPT-J layer.
- The Lu et al. Llama-3.2-3B stages (extraction peaking at layer 21) suggest the answer-extraction site is much later than a layer-5 edit, so "where the fact is read out" and "where an edit is best written" can differ. This ties directly to Q4.

### Gaps
- No readable source gives a causal-tracing layer for Llama-2/3 or Gemma facts. EasyEdit hyperparameter files and the ROME repository would need to be opened to confirm default layers.
- I did not verify Hase et al.'s model list (the abs page does not name them).

## Q4. Known criticisms of ROME-style locate-then-edit relevant to edit-layer choice

### Takeaway
Hase et al. show causal-tracing localization does not predict the best edit layer. Separate work shows ROME implementation issues cause model collapse, which matters for perplexity-damage measurements.

### Cited Findings
- Hase, Bansal, Kim, Ghandeharioun, "Does Localization Inform Editing? Surprising Differences in Causality-Based Localization vs. Knowledge Editing in Language Models", arXiv:2301.04213. Causal tracing localization does not reliably indicate which MLP layer to edit; the results "do not provide any insight into which model MLP layer" would be best. Layer choice predicts edit performance much better than localization does, except for one editing variant where editing performance does relate to localization. Editing at locations other than the traced ones can still change how a fact is stored. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)
- Gupta, Baskaran, Anumanchipalli, "Rebuilding ROME: Resolving Model Collapse during Sequential Model Editing", arXiv:2403.07175. "Disabling edits" that collapse the model come from implementation irregularities. They propose r-ROME, which avoids collapse and improves generalization and locality. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07175)
- Yang, Sun, Tan, Ma, Su, Yin, Shen, "Understanding the Collapse of LLMs in Model Editing" (listed on the page under the title "The Fall of ROME" in the search results), arXiv:2406.11263. Collapse comes from inconsistent handling of prefixed vs unprefixed keys, which produces tiny denominators and huge updates, and affects the first token of the subject. Fix: use prefixed keys during editing and add prefixes at test time. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11263)
- Fang et al. (AlphaEdit, 2410.02355) argue locate-then-edit perturbations disrupt preserved knowledge, especially under sequential edits. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355)

### Inferences
- For the proposed study, the Hase result means a tracing-derived edit layer cannot be assumed best. A fair design sweeps edit layers and reports tracing-layer vs best-layer transfer separately.
- Collapse-inducing ROME edits can inflate perplexity damage. Using r-ROME or prefixed keys, or at least checking for outlier edits, would keep the perplexity metric interpretable.

### Gaps
- I did not find a criticism specific to multilingual locate-then-edit beyond the surveys' remark about language-specific activation patterns and anisotropy (2505.14393 abstract).
- Hase et al. models and per-layer numbers were not read.

## Q5. Closest prior work to EN/ES/HI causal tracing on Llama-3.2-3B, with cross-language circuit overlap vs a shuffled-fact permutation null, then edit transfer, specificity and perplexity damage

### Takeaway
No single paper covers the full design. The closest pieces are 2505.20546 (same model, English and Spanish tracing-style mechanism, no Hindi, no editing), 2406.11139 (ROME/MEMIT with English, Spanish and Hindi, different models, no tracing), and 2410.14387 / 2406.16416 (cross-language mechanism overlap and shared neurons). A shuffled-fact permutation null for overlap was not found in any paper I read.

### Cited Findings
- Closest on model and mechanism: Lu et al., arXiv:2505.20546, Llama-3.2-3B, English, French, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, with layer-level pathway analysis and interventions, no editing and no Hindi. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20546)
- Closest on EN/ES/HI editing: Banerjee et al., arXiv:2406.11139, ROME and MEMIT, English, Spanish, Hindi and five other languages, with reliability, locality, generalization and portability, and per-language consistency. Models are 7B, not Llama-3.2-3B. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11139)
- Closest on cross-language mechanism overlap: Fierro et al., arXiv:2410.14387 (patching shows language-independent subject enrichment and language-dependent extraction) and Zhang et al., arXiv:2406.16416 (language-agnostic factual neurons shared across languages). — [Fierro abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14387), [Zhang abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16416)
- Closest on tracing across languages with a Llama model: Schut et al., arXiv:2502.15603 (Llama-3.1-70B appendix). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15603)
- Closest on the edit-layer-choice criticism: Hase et al., arXiv:2301.04213. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)
- Closest on tracing-then-transfer logic with ROME: Qi et al., arXiv:2310.10378. English ROME edits transferred to high-RankC languages (including Spanish) and not to low-RankC ones, on BLOOM-3b. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10378v5)

### Inferences
- The novel contribution relative to these papers would be joining (a) tracing in EN/ES/HI on one Llama model, (b) a quantified circuit-overlap statistic with a shuffled-fact null, and (c) transfer, specificity and perplexity measured on the same facts.
- A reviewer would likely ask how the study differs from 2406.11139 (same languages, no tracing) and 2505.20546 (same model, no Hindi or editing). The shuffled null is the clear differentiator.
