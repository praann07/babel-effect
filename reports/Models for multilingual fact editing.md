# Add Llama-1B, Qwen3-1.7B and BLOOM-1b7

Add three models to the Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Gemma-2-2B plan, in this order: **Llama-3.2-1B**, **Qwen3-1.7B-Base** (loaded through the `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B` registry name) and **BLOOM-1b7**. Before any porting, run a cheap forward-only pilot that counts how many facts each model gets right in English, Spanish and Hindi, because the n>=50 goal is the binding constraint and no source in the notes measures it. Llama-3.2-1B needs almost no code change and keeps the tokenizer fixed. Qwen3-1.7B adds a new training lineage with Hindi and Spanish listed, but its Hindi tokenization (**5.0 tokens per word, 52.9% byte-fragment tokens**) forces full-answer log-probability scoring and a tokenization-stratified analysis. BLOOM-1b7 has the best Hindi tokenization and the closest ROME precedent (BLOOM-3b), but it needs the most code changes. Reasoning models do not help: the small Qwen3 models are distilled hybrids, a one-token readout never exercises thinking, and the R1 distill has no clean base twin. The notes' top pick, Qwen3.5-2B, has no verified support in TransformerLens 3.9.0, so it is out of scope for this toolchain. Gemma-3-4B, Qwen3-4B and Phi-4-mini do not fit in bf16 on the 8151 MiB GPU. No paper found runs causal tracing on Hindi, or runs ROME/MEMIT on English, Spanish and Hindi with a sub-4B Llama, so the design is novel, and every model-specific claim below that was not executed is labelled UNVERIFIED.

## Prior work agrees on poor transfer but never traced Hindi

Every cross-lingual editing study in the notes finds that weight edits (ROME, MEMIT, KN, FT, MEND) transfer badly across languages, and worse across scripts. The only paper that edits with ROME and MEMIT in English, Spanish and Hindi is Banerjee et al., on 7B Mistral, Tower and Hindi-adapted OpenHathi models, not on a small Llama ([arXiv:2406.11139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11139)). Its reported Spanish self-edit reliability is about **0.81-0.82 for ROME**, but Hindi on OpenHathi is **0.02 for ROME and 0.45 for MEMIT**. An English edit queried in Hindi on the merged Indic model scores **0.00 (ROME) and 0.01 (MEMIT)** ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11139v4)). The notes say these numbers came from a fetch-summarizer, so re-check the tables before quoting them. The paper's layer-5 edit setting is a hyperparameter, not a tracing result. The Chinese-English study reports ROME cross-lingual F1 near 27 against about 93 for in-context editing on one backbone ([arXiv:2309.08952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08952)), again with a possible column-misalignment caveat. Cross-lingual multi-hop editing reports FT, ROME and MEMIT averaging under 5% across eight languages including Hindi and Spanish ([arXiv:2407.10275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10275)). The one ROME case study on BLOOM-3b shows edits propagating to languages that are consistent with English (Spanish) and mostly not to the others ([arXiv:2310.10378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10378)). Cross-lingual editing of BLOOM, mBERT and XLM-R spans English, French, Spanish, Hindi, Gujarati and Bengali, and reports the largest failures across script families ([arXiv:2401.10521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10521)). BMIKE-53 evaluates Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B on 53 languages including Spanish and Hindi, but only with in-context editing ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17764v2)). Another study, on 7B models and 13 languages including Spanish and Hindi, finds script similarity is a major factor in how much knowledge languages share ([arXiv:2408.10646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10646)).

The mechanism papers converge on a three-stage picture, and none of them covers Hindi. Subject enrichment is language-independent and object extraction is language-dependent in XGLM and EuroLLM ([arXiv:2410.14387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14387)). In LLaMA2-7B, concept-space extraction begins near layer 12 and the language transition sits at layer 28 ([arXiv:2504.04264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04264)). On Llama-3.2-3B itself, answer extraction rises from layer 15 and peaks at layer 21, with translation mostly in MLP layers 22-27 across English, French, Spanish and three East Asian languages ([arXiv:2505.20546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20546)). That paper's text and figure captions give slightly different layer numbers for some stages. Causal tracing on large models finds facts in similar layers regardless of language ([arXiv:2502.15603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15603)). Two criticisms bear directly on the design. Tracing peaks do not predict the best edit layer ([arXiv:2301.04213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)), so the edit layer must be swept per model. ROME implementation quirks cause model collapse that inflates perplexity damage ([arXiv:2403.07175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07175), [arXiv:2406.11263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11263)). The notes found no tracing or editing paper using Gemma-3, Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Phi-4-mini, SmolLM3, Sarvam-1 or Tiny Aya. That absence comes from about 8 to 10 searches, so it is not proof. Among small candidates, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-2-2B are the best precedented, through the factuality benchmarks X-FaKT ([arXiv:2502.17955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17955)) and an Indic factual-accuracy study ([arXiv:2504.20022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20022)). The Qwen-family editing precedents are at 7-8B ([arXiv:2506.10800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10800)). The BLOOM precedents are 3B and 7B.

## Hardware, TransformerLens support and Hindi tokenization remove most candidates

Three filters decide the field. The first is memory. TransformerLens stores an untied unembedding copy, so bf16 weights in TL are larger than the Hugging Face figures: Llama-3.2-1B **2.79 GiB**, Qwen3-1.7B **3.78**, BLOOM-1b7 **4.16**, Gemma-2-2B **5.97**, Llama-3.2-3B **6.72** (the working baseline), Qwen3-4B **8.22** and Gemma-3-4B **8.48** ([TL footprint arithmetic](file:///C:/Users/Som%20Praneeth/Desktop/reasearch/babel-effect/.venv/Lib/site-packages/transformer_lens/HookedTransformer.py)). These are arithmetic, not measurements. The GPU has 8151 MiB total with about 553 MiB idle, so Qwen3-4B and Gemma-3-4B cannot be trained against in bf16. The candidate-models note proposed 4-bit Gemma-3-4B, but TL refuses `hf_model` quantization except bitsandbytes 4-bit on names containing "llama" ([HookedTransformer.py](file:///C:/Users/Som%20Praneeth/Desktop/reasearch/babel-effect/.venv/Lib/site-packages/transformer_lens/HookedTransformer.py)). Gemma-3-4B is therefore dropped for this laptop.

The second filter is registry support. The 3.9.0 registry covers Qwen2.5, Qwen3 (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, with `-Base` only for 0.6B), Gemma-2, Gemma-3, Llama-3.2, Phi-3-mini and BLOOM, but not SmolLM2, Granite, Aya or Phi-4-mini ([supported_models.py](file:///C:/Users/Som%20Praneeth/Desktop/reasearch/babel-effect/.venv/Lib/site-packages/transformer_lens/supported_models.py)). The TL note never lists Qwen3.5, Sarvam-1 or Tiny Aya, so their support is UNVERIFIED. Qwen3.5-2B is a hybrid with 18 Gated-DeltaNet layers and only 6 softmax-attention layers, so it cannot map onto the HookedTransformer attention code. Even where a Bridge route exists for other models, the project's hooks and hand-rolled unembedding were written for HookedTransformer. HookedTransformer is deprecated in 3.9.0 but works; pin `transformer_lens==3.9.0`.

The third filter is Hindi tokenization, measured on 240 parallel XQuAD paragraphs. Gemma-2 gives **2.12** tokens per Hindi word with 0.0% byte fragments, Llama-3 gives **2.80** with 0.4%, BLOOM gives **1.43** with 0.0%, and Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 give **5.00 with 52.9%** ([XQuAD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/xquad), [Qwen3-1.7B tokenizer](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B)). Qwen3.5 fixes this (2.32, 0.1%) but fails the second filter ([Qwen3.5-2B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B)). Factual knowledge is the weakest evidence. Llama-3.2-3B's published multilingual MMLU is **55.1 in Spanish and 43.3 in Hindi**, and **41.5 and 33.5** for the 1B model, which is close to chance ([Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct card](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct)). On Sarvam's translated Indic benchmarks Gemma-2-2B scores 29.86 on Indic MMLU against 33.39 for Llama-3.2-3B ([Sarvam-1 blog](https://sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-1)), a vendor report. Gemma-3-1B scores 24.9 on Global-MMLU-Lite, about chance ([Gemma-3-4B-pt card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-4b-pt)). These are aggregate reasoning-style tests, not fact-recall rates, so the yield pilot is the only real answer to the n>=50 question. For planning, treat Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.2-1B as likely to yield few Hindi-correct facts outside very well-known entities such as capitals. Draw a candidate pool of several hundred such facts, then filter per model. Per-model fact sets, with the shared-across-models subset reported separately, avoid letting the weakest model set n for everyone.

## Qwen3 stays, but as a tokenization-stratified secondary arm

The reasoning-models note recommends Qwen3-1.7B-Base as the primary new model. The candidate-models note ranks Qwen3-1.7B third because of its Hindi tokenization. The two notes agree on every fact: Hindi is listed for Qwen3, the model fits, and the tokenizer splits Devanagari into byte fragments. The reasoning note's local pilot independently found a lone byte-fragment token as the top-1 next token for a Hindi prompt (p=0.536). They differ in what they optimize, a clean base-versus-post-trained twin versus a valid Hindi readout. The resolution is a demotion, not an exclusion. First-token probability is not a valid Hindi metric for Qwen3, because the first token of many different Hindi objects is the same meaningless fragment. The Hindi arm must score the full answer string by summed log-probability or multi-token exact match. A byte-fragment last subject token also makes the ROME key vector and the tracing site position-ambiguous, and this is an inference, not a measured effect. So analyze Qwen3 on all facts and on the stratum where the Hindi subject's last token and the object's first token are whole characters, and report both. Giving Qwen3 the primary slot would put a confound exactly where the study's central Hindi-versus-Spanish comparison lives. Qwen3's strengths still earn it second place: multilingual MMMLU 63.27 for the 1.7B base, Hindi in its evaluation sets ([Qwen3 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)), Apache-2.0, and Moir's finding that the Qwen3-8B tracing peak moved from layer 8 (base) to layer 14 (post-trained) while Llama-3.1 and OLMo-2 stayed put ([arXiv:2607.20433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20433)). That finding makes any Qwen3 arm worth tracing separately instead of reusing a layer.

Reasoning models fail the cost-benefit test. Extra reasoning tokens act as a computational buffer and as factual priming ([arXiv:2603.09906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09906)), so thinking-mode behaviour is a multi-token process that last-subject-token patching cannot test. The local pilot on Qwen3-1.7B put p(" Paris")=0.509 on a raw prompt and 12 of 12 correct first tokens on 12 English capitals, but p("The")=1.0 after the chat template with thinking disabled. Run Qwen3 on raw, template-free prompts and keep the template readout as a documented sensitivity check. The small Qwen3 models are produced by strong-to-weak distillation ([Qwen3 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)), so any base-versus-post-trained difference reflects distillation and chat training, not reasoning. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B is built on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B ([model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B)), has no clean twin and forces `<think>`. Its tokenizer fertility was not measured but it belongs to the same Qwen vocabulary family (UNVERIFIED). No paper compares factual-recall circuits of a distill with its base. The nearest evidence is that reasoning fine-tuning reuses base representations ([arXiv:2507.12638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12638)) and that ROME collapses on R1-Distill-Llama-8B after 100 sequential edits ([arXiv:2505.18690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18690)), a sequential-edit result that does not bear on single edits. A cleaner post-training control exists in already-registered pairs with good tokenizers: `google/gemma-2-2b` versus `-it`, and Llama-3.2-3B base versus Instruct. This is my suggestion and has not been run. SmolLM3 lacks Hindi, and Phi-4-mini-reasoning is English-only and math-only ([card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-mini-reasoning)).

## Per-model code changes

The existing code is written around Llama, so each model breaks it in a different place. The table lists the changes. All TL-derived facts come from the installed 3.9.0 sources cited above, and none was executed against a model; every change needs a logit-parity check against Hugging Face before it is trusted.

| Model (TL name) | Layers, d_model | Required changes | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma-2-2B (`google/gemma-2-2b`) | 26, 2304 | Apply `30*tanh(x/30)` in the hand-rolled last-position unembed. Handle the post-MLP RMSNorm in ROME. Replace hard-coded 28 with `model.cfg.n_layers`. Re-layout names as for Llama (GQA, 4 KV heads, `_W_K/_W_V`). Do not share `W_U` with `W_E`. Run a bf16 parity check. | Config hardcoded in TL, offline OK |
| Llama-3.2-1B (`meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B`) | 16, 2048 | Layer count 16 only. Edit-layer range must scale. Same tokenizer and re-layout names. | Same code path, offline OK |
| Qwen3-1.7B-Base (name `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`, `hf_model` and `tokenizer` from the Base repo) | 28, 2048 | No BOS is prepended, so check any position offset that assumes a BOS at index 0. Score Hindi by full-answer log-probability. Cache the hub config first. Expect `trust_remote_code` messages. `_W_K/_W_V` apply. QK-norm leaves MLP hooks unchanged. | Registry name is real; Base via `hf_model` is UNVERIFIED |
| BLOOM-1b7 (`bigscience/bloom-1b7`) | 24, 2048 | Add `ln_final.b` and mean-centering to the manual unembed. Branch the re-layout on a non-gated MLP (no `W_gate`) and MHA (`W_K/W_V`, not `_W_K/_W_V`). `hook_embed` is post-LayerNorm, so the noise scale from `W_E` is wrong. ALiBi and no BOS. | LayerNorm path not read, UNVERIFIED |

For Gemma-2 the soft-cap and the post-norm are the two real changes. TL applies the final-logit soft-cap only inside `HookedTransformer.forward` after `unembed`, so a manual `(h * ln_final.w) @ W_U` skips it. Ranks and argmax are unchanged by the monotonic tanh, but log-probabilities, edit losses and KL change. In Gemma-2 and Gemma-3, `hook_mlp_out` fires after `ln2_post`, so there is no hook on the raw `W_out` output ([transformer_block.py](file:///C:/Users/Som%20Praneeth/Desktop/reasearch/babel-effect/.venv/Lib/site-packages/transformer_lens/components/transformer_block.py)). The TL note's suggested conversion (`pre-norm target = scale_orig * normalised target`) ignores that the scale changes when the vector changes. A cleaner fix, which I have not run, is to wrap `blocks[l].ln2_post` in a module that carries a new `HookPoint` on its input and call `model.setup()`. Optimize the delta there, then form the rank-1 `W_out` update from the raw pre-norm value, so the edited and optimized graphs apply the identical norm. Because the norm discards magnitude, regularize the delta direction. Two further traps are the 1+w norm weights stored in bf16 (quantised against Hugging Face) and the pre-scaled `W_E`, which keeps the corruption-noise estimate consistent. Qwen3-1.7B and Llama-3.2-3B both have 28 layers, so a hard-coded 28 would silently run on Qwen3-1.7B but crash or misindex on Gemma-2 (26), Llama-1B (16) and BLOOM-1b7 (24). The re-layout helper hard-codes `W_gate` and `_W_K/_W_V`, and it would raise `AttributeError` on MHA models and BLOOM. Qwen3-0.6B, a possible debug model, has `n_heads * d_head` = 2048 against `d_model` = 1024, which breaks any shape that assumes they are equal. One other known gotcha is TL's behavior on Qwen: its hub config call hung in the research sandbox. Cache the configs and set `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, though that offline load is UNVERIFIED.

| Rank | Model | Why | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | Llama-3.2-1B | Same tokenizer and code, scale control, 2.79 GiB | Hindi MMLU 33.5 is near chance, so the n>=50 gate may fail |
| 2 | Qwen3-1.7B-Base | New lineage, Hindi and Spanish listed, 3.78 GiB, apache-2.0 | Byte-fragment Hindi, Base through `hf_model` untested |
| 3 | BLOOM-1b7 | 1.43 tokens per Hindi word, BLOOM-3b ROME precedent, 4.16 GiB | Most code change, BLOOM's Hindi and Spanish coverage unconfirmed on a primary page, weak knowledge (UNVERIFIED) |
| 4 (optional) | Qwen3-1.7B post-trained, Gemma-2-2B-it, Llama-3.2-3B base | Post-training controls, raw prompts | Confounded by distillation and chat tuning |
| Out of scope | Qwen3.5-2B, Sarvam-1, Tiny Aya, Gemma-3-4B, Qwen3-4B, Phi-4-mini, SmolLM3, Granite, Qwen2.5, Gemma-3-1B, R1 distills | No verified TL 3.9.0 support, no fit in bf16, no Hindi, or chance-level knowledge | n/a |

BLOOM's presence in the plan rests on indirect evidence: the notes did not confirm its training languages on a primary page, though BLOOM-based cross-lingual editing papers include Hindi and Spanish ([arXiv:2401.10521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10521)). Sarvam-1 is the strongest Hindi model in the notes (Indic TriviaQA 90.62 against 46.00 for Llama-3.2-3B, [blog](https://sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-1)), but Spanish is unsupported and tokenizes at 2.56 tokens per word. It is a registry miss in TL, so it would need an unverified name monkeypatch.

The order of work follows risk. First, build the fact pool and run a forward-only Hugging Face yield pilot for all four models, scoring full-answer log-probability in all three languages, since this cheap step can reorder or cut the list. Second, refactor the harness to read layer counts, d_model and head counts from `model.cfg`, and add a per-model parity test against Hugging Face. Third, add Gemma-2-2B, since it is already planned and exercises the soft-cap and post-norm changes. Fourth, Llama-3.2-1B, then Qwen3-1.7B-Base with the stratified Hindi analysis, and BLOOM-1b7 last. Sweep edit layers on every model per [Hase et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213), and apply r-ROME-style prefixed keys or outlier checks to keep perplexity damage interpretable ([arXiv:2406.11263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11263)). Gate each model on n>=50 before spending porting time.

Several notes disagree on peripheral numbers, and the report uses the safer one. The weight totals differ between the notes (4.87 GiB against 5.97 for Gemma-2-2B), because one counts tied Hugging Face weights and the other TL's untied copy; use TL's. The notes cite transformers 5.18 and 5.19; check which is installed. The Lu et al. layer numbers differ between text and figures. The research also leaves several items UNVERIFIED: no model was loaded, so every memory and parity claim is arithmetic; Qwen3 Base via `hf_model`, offline cached-config loading and the Gemma-2 hook wrapper were not run; Gemma-2 and Llama-3 tracing layers are not published in a readable source; and the Hindi-versus-Spanish fact yield per model is unknown.

## Conclusion

The ranking is driven by a metric problem more than a model-quality problem. Hindi tokenization, not parameter count, decides which models give a valid Hindi readout, and it shows that "which model knows the most Hindi" and "which model can be measured in Hindi" are different questions. The one candidate that scores well on both, Qwen3.5-2B, falls outside the toolchain. The practical advice is to pick the models by yield and measurability, keep the pipeline metric full-answer log-probability throughout, and treat Qwen3 as a stratified secondary arm.

The notes also suggest that post-training is a better handle than "reasoning". A distilled hybrid cannot show a reasoning effect in a one-token readout, but Moir's layer shift for Qwen3-8B shows that post-training can move the tracing peak. The cheapest way to test that on this hardware is to compare base and instruct checkpoints that TL already registers, such as Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-2B-it. This tests whether the cross-lingual localization result survives instruction tuning, which no paper found has measured for Hindi.
