1. THE GOAL
====================================================================
Write a paper good enough for a conference or journal (ACL / EMNLP / ICLR / TMLR). Venue not chosen yet.

Research question: when a language model knows a fact ("capital of France = Paris"), is it stored
separately per language, or once in a shared, language-neutral form?

Method (small open models, one 8 GB laptop GPU, $0 spend, English / Spanish / Hindi):
  1. Causal tracing: scramble the subject, restore clean internal states one layer/word at a time,
     see which layers bring the answer back.
  2. Compare the languages' layer profiles against a shuffled-fact null (1000 permutations).
  3. Edit the fact in English only (rank-1 edit of one MLP layer).
  4. Check whether Spanish and Hindi change too; measure damage to unrelated facts and to text.
Models: Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2B (done); BLOOM-1b7 and Llama-3.2-1B (queued).

3. RESULTS SO FAR (all numbers are in results/manifest.json and docs/LOG.md)
====================================================================
A. Overlap of causal profiles (median MLP-profile cosine, pooled shuffled-fact null; z-score)
   Llama (n=92):  EN-ES 0.627 z=4.22 | EN-HI 0.509 z=2.45 | ES-HI 0.423 z=2.47
   Gemma (n=58):  EN-ES 0.464 z=4.81 | EN-HI 0.226 z=2.19 | ES-HI 0.137 z=0.08
   With a same-relation null, only EN-ES stays significant after correction in both models
   (stratified z: Llama 3.29 / 1.79 / 1.98; Gemma 3.85 / 1.68 / -0.45).
   -> H6 holds: English and Spanish are aligned beyond chance in both models.
B. Hindi: weaker, and depends on how many tokens the Hindi subject takes (median 5 tokens Llama, 8 Gemma,
   vs 2 for English). Llama EN-HI cosine 0.60 with short Hindi subjects vs 0.20 with long ones.
   Hindi accuracy collapses mainly for answers of 7+ tokens.
C. Where the fact is looked up: Llama layers 0-6 (median 3-4); Gemma has three modes (layers 4, 6, 14).
   Layer 14 is the peak for 17/58 English and 13/58 Spanish facts but only 2/58 Hindi facts (exploratory).
D. Edit sweep (English-only edit, 10 facts per model):
   English success 0.84 (Llama) / 0.74 (Gemma) over all layers; edits at the last layers often fail.
   Spanish success 0.41 / 0.48; Hindi 0.18 / 0.20 (never above 0.5 at any layer).
   At the traced layer: Spanish transfer ratio 0.66 (Llama) / 0.38 (Gemma); Hindi 0.0001 / 0.095.
   H7 (mid-layer edits transfer more than layers 0-5 and cost more specificity):
     Gemma  - supported (Spanish +0.34 [0.01, 0.62], Hindi +0.07 [0.03, 0.13], specificity +0.04).
     Llama  - contradicted for Spanish (-0.31 [-0.47, -0.09]); Hindi and specificity not different from zero.
   -> model-dependent; no pooled claim.
E. H9 (EN-ES z>2 in at least two new relations): not supported so far (only event_year testable, z=0.91).
   H8 (phrasing robustness): pending.

Honest assessment: workshop paper likely; Findings-level plausible if the extra runs hold;
main-conference acceptance unlikely. Null and mixed results are reported as results.
