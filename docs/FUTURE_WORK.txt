THE BABEL EFFECT - FUTURE WORK AND KNOWN WEAKNESSES (written 2026-10-21, for the reviewer)
Companion to paper/The_Babel_Effect_draft_v2.pdf. Nothing here is claimed as a result.

A. EXPERIMENTS ALREADY DESIGNED (pre-registered in docs/HYPOTHESIS.md) BUT NOT YET RUN
1. Gemma-2-2B follow-up edits (20 further facts at the rule-chosen layer, the traced layer and L//2). The run was interrupted; the Llama counterpart is done.
2. BLOOM-1b7 (A10): a model that was trained on Hindi with a tokenizer that splits Hindi into fewer pieces. Predictions fixed in advance: B1 at least 50 facts survive the filter; B2 EN-ES z > 2; B3 Hindi subject spans <= 3 tokens and EN-HI z > 2. This is the direct test of whether the weak Hindi result is a tokenisation effect. The TransformerLens BLOOM code path (ALiBi, no BOS token) is untested and may need debugging.
3. Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct pilot (same tokenizer as the main model, smaller size); full pipeline only if at least 50 facts pass the filter.

B. KNOWN WEAKNESSES THAT FUTURE WORK SHOULD FIX (the author's own list, not exhaustive)
1. Sample size. 29 to 126 facts per analysis; edit sweeps use only 10 facts per model and the follow-up 20. Per-layer rates move in steps of 0.1. Needs several hundred facts and a power analysis stated in advance.
2. Hand-written facts and templates. Templates were chosen on the same 30 facts per relation that are later evaluated. Needs held-out template selection, more relations, and facts from a public multilingual benchmark (for example X-FACTR / mLAMA style data) instead of hand-written ones.
3. Model confound. Llama is an Instruct model and Gemma a base model; differences between them cannot be attributed to architecture. Needs base and instruct versions of the same family.
4. Hindi. Hindi subjects span 5 (Llama) and 8 (Gemma) tokens against 2 for English, and the EN-HI overlap falls with span length. Hindi facts and answers were not checked by a native speaker. Needs a Hindi-speaker audit and a language with a better tokenisation (BLOOM, Qwen) as controls.
5. What "overlap" measures. The main statistic is the cosine of two per-layer MLP profiles at one token. Similar depth profiles could arise for any two prompts about the same kind of fact. Needs (i) a control comparing different facts in the same language against different languages of the same fact, (ii) multi-token and attention-head patching, (iii) path patching, not only single-site restoration.
6. Single noise level and single corruption scheme (Gaussian noise, 3 sigma on subject tokens). Needs sensitivity to the noise level and to a symmetric (token-swap) corruption.
7. Edit method. Only a rank-1 edit of one MLP layer is used; no MEMIT-style multi-layer edit, no sequential edits. Edit success is defined by greedy generation of the exact new answer.
8. Specificity uses 20 control facts and damage uses 100 Wikipedia sentences; both are small. Mid-layer edits showed large specificity loss in the Llama follow-up (median 0.57 against 0.06 at the traced layer); the 10-fact sweep gave 0.08, so the estimate is unstable.
9. In-context baseline anomaly. In Llama, an English statement in the prompt changed Spanish and Hindi answers for 10/10 facts but English for 0/10. This was not investigated and may be a formatting or token-boundary issue; the baseline has only 10 facts per model.
10. Post-hoc analyses. The relation-stratified null, the Hindi span split and the critical-layer histogram (Gemma modes at layers 4, 6, 14) were added after seeing results and are labelled exploratory.
11. H7 (mid-layer edits transfer more) is supported in Gemma and contradicted for Spanish in Llama; no pooled claim is made and the Llama follow-up does not re-test it.
12. Two model families only; no model above 3B parameters.
13. Related work was searched in a bounded way; the claim that no paper combines tracing, overlap, and cross-lingual edit transfer on the same model is an absence in that search, not proof.

C. PLANNED IMPROVEMENTS IF THE PAPER CONTINUES
1. Finish A and rerun the paper's tables with the full results.
2. Add a power analysis and expand to several hundred facts across more relations and a public benchmark.
3. Add base/instruct pairs of one family and one larger model (7B class) if compute allows (current limit: one 8 GB GPU, no spend).
4. Add the controls in B5 and B6 and report whether the EN-ES alignment survives them.
5. Native-speaker audit of the Hindi data.
6. Explain or fix the in-context anomaly (B9).
7. Clean-clone reproduction of the whole pipeline (reproduce.sh currently covers the verification of 537 numbers, not the full v2 pipeline).
8. Choose a venue; current honest estimate: workshop paper likely, Findings plausible if A and B1-B6 hold up, main-conference acceptance unlikely.

D. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE AUTHOR'S CONCLUSION
- If EN-ES overlap disappears under the control in B5 (different facts, same language pair), the "shared lookup" claim is wrong.
- If BLOOM-1b7 shows strong EN-HI overlap with short Hindi spans, the weak Hindi result is mostly tokenisation. If it does not, tokenisation is not the explanation.
- If edit transfer to Spanish and Hindi stays low even where the in-context baseline transfers, the edit site (not cross-lingual access) is the bottleneck.
